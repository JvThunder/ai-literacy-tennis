# Forehand Technique Guide for Beginners
Source: club coaching handbook (sample document)

## Ready position and grip
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees softly bent and the racket held in front with both hands. A semi-western or eastern forehand grip lets the strings meet the ball flat while the wrist stays relaxed. Beginners often grip too tightly; hold the racket at about a four out of ten effort so the arm can swing freely.

## Unit turn and shoulder rotation
The forehand starts with the unit turn: the shoulders and hips rotate together so the chest faces the side fence and the non-racket hand guides the racket back. A full shoulder rotation stores energy in the trunk and lets the body, not just the arm, accelerate the racket. A swing with little rotation relies on the arm alone and loses both power and consistency.

## Preparation timing
Finish the turn before the ball bounces. Early preparation gives time to set the feet and choose where to make contact. Late preparation squeezes the swing and pushes contact behind the hip, which causes mishits and balls that drift wide.

## Contact point
Contact the ball in front of the front hip, roughly an arm and racket length from the body, at about waist height. Contact beside or behind the hip forces the wrist to compensate and makes direction unpredictable. A contact point out in front also lets the racket travel through the ball toward the target.

## Arm spacing at contact
The hitting arm should be relaxed and slightly bent at contact, close to the elbow angle expert players show rather than fully straight. A cramped arm means the player is too close to the ball and jams the swing. A locked straight arm removes feel and shortens the useful swing path.

## Follow-through
After contact the racket keeps travelling forward, up and across the body, finishing near the opposite shoulder. A full follow-through is a sign the racket accelerated through the ball. A short, abrupt finish usually means the player slowed down before contact, which reduces depth and control.

## Balance and posture
Keep the head still and the trunk close to vertical from preparation to contact. Leaning away from the ball or tipping forward moves the racket off its intended path. Finish the stroke in balance and hold the finish for a moment as a self-check.

## Legs and weight transfer
Bend the knees slightly during preparation and push up and forward through the stroke. A small knee bend lowers the centre of gravity, helps with low balls and allows weight to move toward the target. Standing tall with straight legs makes the shot arm-dominated and unstable.
