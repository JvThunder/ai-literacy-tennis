# Common Beginner Forehand Faults
Source: coach education notes (sample document)

## Fault: arm-only swing
Players who do not rotate their shoulders hit with the arm alone. The swing looks compact, but the ball comes off slowly and unpredictably. The usual cause is turning the racket back instead of turning the body. Fix it with shadow turns that bring the non-racket hand to the side fence before any ball is fed.

## Fault: late contact
When the ball is struck beside or behind the hip the racket face is open or closed by the time it arrives. Late contact is usually a preparation problem, not a swing speed problem. Start the turn as soon as the ball leaves the opponent's racket and mark a contact zone in front of the front hip.

## Fault: short follow-through
Stopping the racket soon after contact is a sign the player is guiding the ball rather than swinging through it. Beginners often do this out of fear of hitting long. Aiming one metre above the net and finishing over the opposite shoulder teaches the swing to continue.

## Fault: cramped or locked elbow
A hitting arm that is jammed against the body or fully straight reduces the margin for error. The cause is almost always footwork: the player did not move to a comfortable distance from the ball. Fix spacing with fed balls at varied depth, asking the player to adjust the feet first.

## Fault: staying tall
Straight legs reduce balance and make low balls difficult. Many beginners bend at the waist instead of the knees. Cue a small sit into the legs during the turn and a push up through contact.

## Fault: leaning away
Tipping the trunk away from the ball at contact pulls the head and racket off line. It often appears when players try to generate power by throwing the body at the ball. Keep the head still and let the racket swing past it.

## Fault: overhitting for power
Swinging harder rarely helps a beginner. Power comes from rotation, timing and a relaxed arm. If the ball is going long or into the net, reduce effort and focus on contact point and finish.
