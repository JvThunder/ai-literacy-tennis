# Filming Your Swing and Weekly Planning
Source: programme guide (sample document)

## Filming from the side
Place the phone at hip height about four to five metres to the side or behind the hitting arm so the whole body, from head to feet, is in frame. A steady phone and good light help the pose estimator track the joints. Record one clear forehand per clip.

## Filming from the front
A camera in front of the player is convenient but hides shoulder rotation because the turn happens toward and away from the lens. Measurements of rotation from a front view are less reliable and should be treated as tentative.

## Planning around time and court access
Two or three short sessions a week work better than one long one. Players without court access can still rehearse the turn, contact marker and finish with shadow swings at home. Reserve court time for fed or self-dropped balls.

## Session structure
A twenty-minute session can hold one main drill of about ten minutes and a short secondary drill of five minutes. Finish with a few free forehands to apply the focus in a more game-like way.

## Reviewing the week
After each session note whether the drills were completed, how hard they felt and whether the feedback helped. Repeat drills that were skipped, simplify those that were too hard and progress those that felt too easy.

## Re-filming to check improvement
Record a new clip after a week of practice from the same angle. Compare the measured values against the previous clip. A change in the main issue is a better indicator of progress than whether the shot feels better.
